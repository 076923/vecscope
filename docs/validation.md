# Held-out validation on public data

Every dataset and model on this page is public, and none of the datasets was used to set a threshold: the defaults were fitted on SciFact, NFCorpus and ko-strategyqa ([verification](verification.md)). Generated by `examples/validation/report.py` from the results of `examples/validation/run.py`; the thresholds were not changed to fit these numbers.

A pipeline is a (dataset, model) pair. It is **good** when its exact-search nDCG@10 is at least 90% of the best model on that dataset and **bad** at 50% or less; the rest are left out of the counts. Datasets with fewer than 5 models are left out. A **false positive** is a finding that is not `ok` on a good pipeline (the deprecated `ood_queries` is ignored). A **miss** is a bad pipeline with every finding `ok`.

266 pipelines, 19 datasets, 14 models; 114 good, 67 bad.

At the default gate (`--fail-on fail`) 26 of the 114 good pipelines fail and 26 have every finding `ok`; 54 of the 67 bad pipelines fail. 54 of the bad pipelines are English-only models on other languages. `collapse` on a corpus whose texts are themselves duplicated (14 pipelines) is counted as correct, not as a false positive. `query_hubness` was not judged at warn on 49 pipelines: chance alone reaches warn_at with that few queries over that many documents.

What these numbers mean for the defaults, and what to do about the false positives, is in [verification](verification.md#held-out-public-data).

## Injected faults

`examples/validation/faults.py` injects one fault at a time into every good pipeline on a corpus of at most 25,000 documents and audits it with its queries and qrels. A fault is **harmful** when exact nDCG@10 drops by more than 0.01, the retrieval tolerance below, so the retrieval column catches every harmful fault by construction; it needs labeled queries, and the other columns show what the findings catch without them. Caught by: **absolute**, the verdict becomes `fail` (it was not on the clean run); **diff**, a verdict is worse than on the clean run as baseline (`--fail-on none --baseline`); **findings**, either of the two; **retrieval**, nDCG@10 fell by more than 0.01 against the baseline (`--retrieval-tolerance ndcg_at_k=0.01`). On harmless faults the same columns count alarms.
